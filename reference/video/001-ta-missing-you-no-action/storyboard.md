## Shooting Setup

- Background: dark cloth or matte table surface, no busy pattern.
- Props: tarot deck, black stone, red thread, clear crystal, small lamp or side light.
- Camera A: vertical 45-degree shot with reader's hands, torso, table, and a little face/lower face if comfortable.
- Camera B: vertical overhead close-up for shuffle, cut, card turn, and spread.
- Camera C: macro detail for anchors, reversed cards, and finger pauses.
- Audio: use the full script as natural voiceover or direct-to-camera reading.
- On-screen text: only section names, landing points, card position, and 1-2 key lines per group.

## Draw Process

Show an actual draw process so the reading does not feel pre-baked:

1. Begin with one shuffled deck in frame.
2. Split into three facedown piles with anchors already visible.
3. For each group, turn six cards one by one while naming the position.
4. Keep the drawn cards visible during interpretation.
5. If a card is reversed, rotate it visibly before placing it.

## Edit Rules

- The viewer should feel a real reader is looking at the cards, not filling a template.
- Do not overdecorate the table. The anchors and cards are enough.
- Keep cuts gentle. The reading depends on trust more than spectacle.
- When the cards are difficult, do not soften them into a sweet answer.
- For 小红书, put chapter times in body copy and pinned comment; if jump cards are available, map them to the exact landing points.
- Do not use the preview frames as final video scenes. They are only for planning and checking the order of information.
